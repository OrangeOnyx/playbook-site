The Four Levels

The program is delivered in four levels. Each level is complete in itself, ends with its own graduation, and earns the right to the next. Depth and autonomy increase with each level; so does the evidence required.

Choosing your level

Start at Starter. Almost everyone should, regardless of experience. Starter is not a remedial tier, it is the proof that one real workflow can run through your AI with guardrails, and it produces the person-level document every later module inherits from. No new tools, no automation. Its only cost is honesty about one workflow.

Choose Intermediate as your finish line if your goal is a system that remembers: your sources, decisions, commitments, your week. Intermediate is the right permanent home for most participants. It installs the memory layer, capture routes, boot sequence, and weekly review, the infrastructure that makes every later capability possible.

Choose Expert when you are ready to delegate recurring work to a bounded operator, a scoped assistant performing a real job on a cadence, inside enforced limits, under observation. Expert is gated by the Security Baseline module. You cannot graduate with an unenforceable boundary, an undrilled kill switch, or an unrestored backup. Governed delegation without governance is just risk with better formatting.

Choose Frontier Operator when you have proven, instrumented workflows and want to automate what the evidence justifies, and to make the system portable, survivable, and reviewable. It lands inside the 66-day habit window on purpose: by graduation, the operating behaviors have had long enough to become automatic. Some automation maps will end in a documented decision to remain assisted. That is a pass, not a failure.

Upgrading between levels

Each level’s graduation produces the entry conditions for the next. Nothing is repeated:

  • Starter → Intermediate: your “How I operate” page, context brief, SOP, and evidence log carry forward unchanged.
  • Intermediate → Expert: the boot sequence, memory pipeline, and weekly review are the prerequisites the bounded operator is built on.
  • Expert → Frontier Operator: the security baseline and observed-trial run log become the foundation for automation decisions.

You may stop permanently at any level with a complete, tested system. You may return later: the owner and review dates on every page exist so a system paused at Intermediate in March resumes at Expert in September without reconstruction. What you may not do is skip. Each gate exists because the next level’s risks are real.
